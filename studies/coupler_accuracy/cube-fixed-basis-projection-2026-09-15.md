# Fixed Gaussian basis: bounded cube wake intervention

The finite-core cube state at reduced time 6 contains a discrepancy between
reconstructed Gaussian vorticity and the curl of its induced velocity. This
study fits only the persistent VPM-owned particle strengths toward a padded
Helmholtz projection. It leaves all particle centres, cores, volumes, and the
physical FVM renewal belt unchanged. This is an isolated algorithm candidate,
not a qualified production change or a completed coupled simulation.

## Matched intervention

The accepted saved state has 43,479 three-dimensional Gaussian particles,
`h=0.06 D`, and `sigma=0.066 D`. The cube occupies `[-0.5,0.5]^3 D`, the
near-body FVM faces lie at `+/-1.5 D`, and the physical renewal bounds are
`[-1.385,1.385]^3 D`. The fit holds the 23,320 particles inside those renewal
bounds fixed and changes only the 20,159 outside. Nine raw Gaussian moment
constraints preserve net vortex strength and linear/angular impulse; float32
storage errors are of order `1e-9` in their respective dimensional units.
The first fit takes 17 CG iterations and 1.19 s of CPU time on this machine.

The VPM-only replays begin from the same t=6 accepted checkpoint, use identical
native fine-FVM donor velocities at t=6 and t=7 for renewal, and interpolate
those donor states linearly between endpoints. They advance the historical
Metal/f32 Gaussian VPM solver with the same `dt=0.01 s`, Gaussian cores, body
panels, particle renewal and numerical schemes. No FVM boundary solve or
coupled feedback occurs. The comparison uses full VPM velocity and the fine
FVM native velocity on 392 spatial points spanning seven y and seven z levels.
The renewal seam has 196 points at `1.25 <= x/D <= 1.62`; the outer wake has
98 at `x/D > 1.62`. All RMS velocities are dimensional m/s; the case has
`U_infinity=1 m/s`, so the same numbers are fractions of freestream speed.

The t=7 initial-fit comparison reduces seam error by 12.7% and outer error
by 15.7%. The t=7 seam reflection-asymmetry RMS falls from 0.07289 to
0.05091 m/s, and outer reflection asymmetry from 0.05770 to 0.04001 m/s.
The periodic replay independently confirms a smaller additional improvement
at t=6.2. Its second fit takes 1.12 s, changes outer strengths by 2.40% in
norm, changes no strength inside the renewal belt, and leaves the three stored
raw moment errors below `3.5e-9`. The t=6.1 transfer net-vortex-strength
ledger matches the one-shot control within `8.5e-9 m^3/s` per Cartesian
component. These are short VPM-only screens, not force or clean-start results.
The frozen full-body screen at t=6 gives complete seam error
`0.08202 -> 0.07700 m/s`; the change in the separately refreshed body-panel
velocity is only `5.87e-6 m/s` RMS. Thus the frozen gain is present in the
complete VPM velocity, and the body correction does not account for it.

## Physics gates and a falsification control

Removing a velocity-invisible longitudinal vorticity component should not
require preserving its **raw** enstrophy. On the same padded grid, the outer
fit reduces longitudinal enstrophy from 0.3285 to 0.1062 m³/s² while changing
the solenoidal part from 99.6924 to 99.6833 m³/s², a 0.009% shift. The
production Gaussian Fourier audit instead measures raw enstrophy
`99.9698 -> 99.7388 m³/s²`; its `1e-4` relative gate rejects that change.
The fit also changes total Fourier helicity `0.17669 -> 0.10571 m^4/s²`
and total Fourier kinetic energy `2.25883 -> 2.25821 m^5/s²`.
These are substantial against the current solver gates. The study has not
relaxed or bypassed them in production.
Frozen screens at six earlier accepted checkpoints show when the VPM-owned
outer population develops: zero particles at t=1, 224 at t=2, 3,925 at t=3,
8,491 at t=4, 14,699 at t=5, and 20,159 at t=6. The independent outer-fit
strength change rises from 1.51% at t=2 to 2.12%, 3.06%, 4.66% and 5.40%
at t=3–6. Its un-restored raw-enstrophy change already exceeds the current
`1e-4` gate at t=2; the correction norm exceeds the current 2% gate from
t=3 onward. These are one-time corrections to uncorrected historical states.
They do not establish that smaller periodic corrections from a clean start
would fail, because the production method performs additional quadratic
restoration and line searches.
[Early-state gate trajectory](results/cube-wake-drift-2026-09-15/fixed-basis-projection/early_gate_trajectory.json).
In this symmetric cube state, the fine FVM local mean `u dot omega` is nearly
zero at the sampled authority and seam regions. The fixed-basis fit moves the
VPM means toward that reference; seam local helicity-error RMS falls from
0.29258 to 0.27666 m/s². Native fine vorticity and VPM analytic curl use
different reconstructions, so this is a local consistency observation, not
a global helicity-invariant target.
[Local fine-reference check](results/cube-wake-drift-2026-09-15/fixed-basis-projection/local_helicity_t6.json).

A separate compact, genuinely helical manufactured 3D field tests whether the
fit necessarily erases physical helicity. Adding a sampled curl-free component
leaves the represented helicity at 39.32492 m⁴/s². After fitting it is
39.31746 m⁴/s², a 0.019% change, while longitudinal enstrophy drops from
24.3364 to 0.23975 m³/s². Solenoidal enstrophy changes by 0.025% and energy
by 0.22%. This establishes useful selectivity in this smooth manufactured
case; it does not qualify arbitrary body flows or the current strict gates.
[Manufactured record](results/cube-wake-drift-2026-09-15/fixed-basis-projection/helical_manufactured.json).

An outer-only quadratic restoration can return the cube state's total Fourier
energy and helicity to float64 roundoff while retaining the nine moments and
the unchanged renewal belt. The Helmholtz residual ratio remains 0.508
(versus 0.484 for the un-restored fit). However, the t=6 seam particle-plus-
freestream reference error rises from 0.07697 to 0.08023 m/s, near the
0.08198 m/s baseline. Solenoidal enstrophy changes by 0.028%, and the
total strength correction is still 5.36%, above the current 2% limit. The
body panel is omitted from **this frozen restoration comparison**; the
reference-driven replays above include it. This falsifies a simple
energy/helicity restoration as a qualified cure for the cube wake.
[Restoration record](results/cube-wake-drift-2026-09-15/fixed-basis-projection/helicity_restoration_t6.json)
and [enstrophy split](results/cube-wake-drift-2026-09-15/fixed-basis-projection/integrals_t6.json).

## Current performance and qualification limit

The preserved coupled warm-run diagnostics at steps 600–800 report a mean
19.22 s and median 15.80 s per accepted step: median VPM 4.87 s, FVM
5.18 s, transfer 4.82 s, and VPM boundary evaluation 0.82 s. The fine
full-mesh run's first and last native output timestamps span 6459.16 s
across 3009 steps, approximately 2.15 s per step including output and
startup. The present coupled mean is about 9.0 times that reference
estimate, and its median is about 7.4 times. The repeated study fit would
add roughly 0.11 s per coupled macro step if performed every ten steps;
that estimate is not a measured integrated coupled runtime. The algorithm
still fails the requirement that the coupled step be faster than the fine
full-mesh step.

A clean-start or earlier-start coupled qualification remains necessary to
assess velocity, forces, boundary
response, invariant histories and total runtime. The existing cold four-rank
one-step restart study did not finish an FVM solve promptly; it provides no
coupled accuracy or speed evidence. No production coupler, VPM or tutorial
setup has been changed by this study.
