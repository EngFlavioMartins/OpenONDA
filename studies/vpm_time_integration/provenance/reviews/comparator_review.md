# Coupled versus sequential VPM time integration

Assessment date: 10 September 2026. The numerical evidence is generated by
[`assessment.py`](vpm-time-integration-assessment/assessment.py), which is
standalone and does not import or modify the production solver.

## Verdict

For smooth, genuinely cross-coupled advection/stretching ODEs, common-stage
SSPRK3 is justified when accuracy per accepted step is the priority. The
linear probes and the synthetic fixed-core two-particle probe show third-order
convergence for the coupled method, first-order convergence for the historical
sequential method, and second-order convergence for the symmetric split. At
the finest measured synthetic-particle step, the coupled error is `6.29e-9`,
versus `3.43e-4` for the historical sequence and `5.53e-7` for Strang.

This is not a universal stability or cost theorem. On the oscillator, the
coupled SSPRK3 imaginary-axis boundary is `q = omega*dt = sqrt(3)`, while the
exact-subflow Lie and Strang maps are power-bounded for fixed `0 < q < 2`
(with a repeated boundary eigenvalue at `q = 2`). Thus a split map can avoid
exponential modal growth in a band where SSPRK3 amplifies, but its generally
non-orthogonal matrices do not preserve the physical Euclidean amplitude.
Splitting defects vanish or reduce when the commutator is zero or small, but
that fact alone does not imply an accuracy or work win; the choice also depends
on subflow costs, error tolerances and the relevant VPM CFL/strain regime. The
production choice therefore needs measured backend cost; these tests do not
certify rotor stability or explain the late rotor behavior.

## Mathematical comparison

Let `y=(x, alpha)` and write the inviscid method-of-lines system as

\[
  \dot y = A(y)+B(y),\qquad
  A(y)=(u(x,\alpha),0),\qquad
  B(y)=(0,s(x,\alpha)).
\]

Here `s` is the selected stretching rate, for example a contraction of the
velocity gradient with the vortex-strength vector. Assume the fields are
smooth enough for the displayed derivatives, locally Lipschitz on the solution
region, and that the exact subflows exist over one step. Define the vector-field
bracket used in this note by

\[
  [A,B](y) = B'(y)A(y)-A'(y)B(y).
\]

Expanding the exact Lie product that first advances A and then B gives

\[
\phi_B^h\phi_A^h(y) = y+h(A+B)
 +h^2\left[\tfrac12A'A+B'A+\tfrac12B'B\right]+O(h^3),
\]

whereas the exact combined flow is

\[
\phi_{A+B}^h(y) = y+h(A+B)
 +\tfrac12h^2(A'A+A'B+B'A+B'B)+O(h^3).
\]

The one-step defect is therefore

\[
  \phi_B^h\phi_A^h(y)-\phi_{A+B}^h(y)
  = \tfrac12h^2[A,B](y)+O(h^3).
\]

The Lie composition is first-order globally when the bracket is nonzero. If
the bracket vanishes, or is small in the operating region, the leading defect
vanishes or is reduced; this is the main regime in which sequential splitting
can be competitive.

The symmetric Strang composition used in the assessment is

\[
  \phi_A^{h/2}\phi_B^h\phi_A^{h/2}.
\]

The `h^2` defect cancels; its local defect is `O(h^3)`, made from nested
brackets such as `[A,[A,B]]` and `[B,[A,B]]`, and its global order is two.
RK4 substeps make the subflow discretization higher order than this splitting
defect. Common-stage SSPRK3 applies one RK tableau to the full state and has
local defect `O(h^4)` and global order three under the same smoothness
assumptions. These are consistency/order statements, not a claim that its
absolute-stability region contains a split method's region.

## Exact two-way linear systems

The oscillator uses

\[
 A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
 B=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\-\omega^2&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
 M=A+B,
\]

so `x'=alpha`, `alpha'=-omega^2*x`, with exact oscillatory solutions.
The growing probe changes the lower-left sign to `+1`, giving the same
genuinely two-way coupling with eigenvalues (+1) and (-1). The initial
state is `[1, 0]` for both systems, and errors use the analytic solution rather
than a numerical reference.

With `q=omega*h`, the exact subflows for the oscillator give

\[
 G_L=e^{hB}e^{hA}
 =\begin{bmatrix}1&h\\-\omega^2h&1-q^2\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad \det G_L=1,\quad \operatorname{tr}G_L=2-q^2,
\]

and

\[
 G_S=e^{hA/2}e^{hB}e^{hA/2}
 =\begin{bmatrix}
  1-q^2/2 & h(1-q^2/4)\\
  -\omega^2h & 1-q^2/2
 \end{bmatrix}.
\]

Both split matrices have unit-determinant elliptic eigenvalues for `0<q<2`.
For each fixed `q` in this interval, their powers are bounded and there is no
exponential modal growth, although they have phase error. The matrices are
generally non-orthogonal, so the physical Euclidean norm can oscillate and is
not preserved by a one-step map; equivalently, the bounded quantity is a
`q`-dependent modified quadratic invariant rather than physical amplitude.
For example, at `q=1.9` and initial state `[1,0]`, the one-step Euclidean norms
are `2.14709` (Lie) and `2.06350` (Strang), despite both spectral radii being
`1.0`. At `q=2` the repeated eigenvalue is a boundary case; bounded powers are
lost by the associated Jordan defect.

For the combined oscillator matrix, SSPRK3 has stability polynomial

\[
 R(z)=1+z+\tfrac12z^2+\tfrac16z^3,
 \qquad |R(iq)|^2=1-\tfrac1{12}q^4+\tfrac1{36}q^6.
\]

It damps nonzero imaginary modes for `q<sqrt(3)` and amplifies them for
q>\sqrt3. This predicts the measured probe: at `q=1.645` its spectral radius
is `0.9698`; at `q=1.819` it is `1.0457`; the historical and Strang split
maps remain at spectral radius `1.0` there. That spectral-radius statement does
not assert preservation of the physical Euclidean norm. At `q=2.1`, both split
maps have spectral radius `1.8773`, so the wider stable band is not unlimited.

The growing system is used to separate physical amplification from numerical
instability. It has a true `e^t` mode, so growth itself is not a failure. The
reported metric is error against the exact hyperbolic solution and the ratio of
numerical to exact terminal norm; it does not label the physical growing mode
as “unstable.”

## Measurements

All methods use float64, the same RHS matrices, initial states and horizons.
The linear sweep uses `dt = 0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025`; the oscillator horizon
is 8 and the growing-system horizon is 4. The script instruments the actual
RHS calls in every run: three per step for coupled SSPRK3, six for the
historical sequence, and twelve for Strang (4 + 4 + 4 calls for its three
classical-RK4 subflows). It also records full-state versus A/B subproblem call
counts in the raw rows. These are algorithmic evaluator counts, not production
wall-time estimates; a full velocity-plus-gradient production traversal and a
subproblem traversal need not have the same backend cost. The raw rows are in
[`results.csv`](vpm-time-integration-assessment/results.csv) and the complete
metadata/matrices are in [`results.json`](vpm-time-integration-assessment/results.json).

| System and `dt` | Coupled SSPRK3 | Historical Lie | Strang RK4 subflows |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| oscillator, 0.10 | 3.332e-4 | 4.621e-2 | 3.384e-3 |
| oscillator, 0.025 | 5.208e-6 | 1.216e-2 | 2.114e-4 |
| growing, 0.10 | 1.539e-4 | 3.647e-2 | 2.369e-3 |
| growing, 0.025 | 2.553e-6 | 8.909e-3 | 1.484e-4 |
| particles, 0.0125 | 5.063e-8 | 6.850e-4 | 2.212e-6 |
| particles, 0.00625 | 6.292e-9 | 3.430e-4 | 5.529e-7 |

The observed fine-pair rates are approximately 3.0/1.0/2.0 for coupled,
historical and Strang respectively on the particle probe. The linear growing
probe gives 2.97/1.01/2.00; the oscillator gives 3.00/0.99/2.00 at the fine
pair. The work plots show the expected trade: the coupled method uses fewer
RHS evaluations and has lower error throughout this smooth cross-coupled
probe, while the split method's stability band is wider for the oscillator.
The exact-subflow matrices in the stability section are an analytic stability
comparison, not a claim that production code gets exact linear subflows for
free. The convergence runs intentionally use classical RK4 for each Strang
subflow, so its measured count is 12 calls per macro-step; a same-tableau
SSPRK3 Strang variant would be a different nine-call experiment and is not
silently substituted here.

The fixed-core particle system is a synthetic two-particle ODE with positions
and non-parallel strength vectors. Every method uses the same direct smooth
Gaussian-like evaluator

\[
 u_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(\Gamma_j\times r_{ij})
       \exp(-\lVert r_{ij}\rVert^2/(2\sigma^2)),
 \qquad \dot\Gamma_i=(\nabla u_i)^T\Gamma_i,
\]

with `sigma=0.8`, float64 arithmetic and no deletion, remeshing, diffusion or
stabilization. The initial velocity norm is `0.5498` and stretching-rate norm
is `0.3125`, confirming both directions of coupling are active in this toy.
A Strang RK4 result at `dt=1e-4` is compared with a second result at `5e-5`;
their relative difference is `1.06e-10`. The sum of particle strengths is
reported only as a diagnostic; this deliberately selected RHS is not asserted
to conserve it. The production Gaussian/Winckelmans kernel, device precision,
treecode/FMM traversal and particle lifecycle are not used here, so these
evaluator counts must not be read as production timings or physical VPM
validation.

## Reproduction and remaining evidence gap

From the repository root:

```sh
python docs/reviews/vpm-time-integration-assessment/assessment.py
```

This regenerates the raw results and the two figures in under the bounded
standalone experiment scope. The evidence establishes the order and the
linear stability contrast for the stated systems, and it exercises a synthetic
two-particle fixed-core coupling. It does not compare the production treecode
or FMM backend, finite-precision device kernels, VLM shedding, LES/diffusion,
particle insertion/deletion, or the late rotor run. Those effects must not be
inferred from this note.
